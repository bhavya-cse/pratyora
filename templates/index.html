Text that was analyzed

An evidence score, not a final verdict. Pratyora searches fact-checkers, official sites and trusted news outlets, then scores how strongly they back or contradict the claim. Very new or local stories may not be indexed yet, so always open the listed sources. When no trusted source is found, the score is only the wording model's estimate.

From claim to estimate in four steps

Whatever you send in becomes plain text. A wording model scores it the same way every time, and the claim is then checked against fact-checkers and trusted sources, so that part can change as news and search results change.

1

You submit it

Paste text, upload a screenshot or paste a news link. Files and links are checked for size, format and safety first.

2

Pratyora reads it

Screenshots go through OCR. Links are opened and the headline and article text are extracted. Text in Indian languages is translated to English first.

3

It scores and checks it

A TF-IDF and logistic regression model compares the wording with about 24,000 labeled English news articles. Pratyora also searches fact-checkers and trusted news and official sites for the main claim, and a small local model judges whether each source supports it, contradicts it or is unrelated.

4

You decide

You get percentages, a confidence level, the sources found and the exact text analyzed. If no trusted source confirms the claim, it is shown as Unverified, never as Likely Real.

What Pratyora can and can't tell you

A tool like this is only worth trusting if it tells you where it stops.

It can

  • Show how closely wording matches known real and fake articles.
  • Search fact-checkers and trusted outlets and show which sources support or contradict a claim.
  • Surface the exact text it read from images and links.
  • Flag when the evidence is too thin to estimate.

It can't

  • Prove a claim true or false. No sources found does not mean the claim is false.
  • Score unusual or local stories reliably: the wording model learned from English news articles.
  • Judge images, edits or context beyond the words.
  • Replace a newsroom, a source check or your own judgment.
प्रत्यक्षpratyakṣa

Named for direct observation

In Indian epistemology, pratyakṣa is knowledge gained by looking for yourself, before inference or hearsay. Pratyora applies the same habit to the news you read: examine the evidence first, then form a belief.

It is an educational project that shows its working, its limits and its uncertainty.

Privacy

Uploads and pasted links are processed on the server. For a link, the server opens the page you gave it to read the article text. To check a claim, a short extract of it is sent to web search and fact-check services, and non-English text may be sent to a translation service.
